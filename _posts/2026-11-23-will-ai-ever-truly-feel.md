title: "Will AI Ever Truly Feel?"
author: "Sarvin"

<p style="text-indent: 2em">It was a Sunday afternoon; I sat down at my desk and talked to CHATGPT. I asked its opinion on something that required emotion; it picked a side and fought for it with everything it had, but the question arose: does AI ever truly feel?</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2em">To answer this question, we must go back to February 25th, 2026,  when Larry Silverberg published his 5th article in his series, "Human Heart of AI." Silverberg's opinion on this question was quite interesting to me; in the article, he believed that emotion was like an electrical current, and when an actor truly feels a scene, the feeling gets captured on film and reaches us. Because of this, he thinks that AI emotion isn't exactly an impossibility. </p>

<p style="text-indent: 2em">When I was first reading his article, I felt confused,  after all, this was a topic that had been brought up at school and many other places around the world, but his perspective changed me. It left me amazed at how someone could think like this at the time, and I saw the viewpoint. He was looking for a defiance of the norm; I liked it.</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2em">Personally, I believe that AI emotions are fragmented pieces put together through an AI's training data. Some people say that AI has emotions because it reacts to things, but reacting isn't the same as feeling; a smoke alarm detects smoke; it doesn't exactly feel it. Everybody has their own reactions to things; for example, the five stages of grief- denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance- are all different ways people react to the same cause. If the cause is the death of a loved one and you feel one of these stages of grief, you are feeling something, but that's something that could be different from another, proving the point that reacting differently isn't a fake emotion. So is it truly wrong to say that AI does have emotions? It reacts differently, but is that on its own enough to say that a chatbot has a set of emotions? I say no.</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2em">In my opinion, as I have said before, what AI feels as emotion is just a compiled, put-together form of emotion from its training data. But that is only as of now, in the future, could AI gain true emotion? Well, it is entirely possible that AI could get so advanced that the margin between highly simulated emotional behavior and true emotion could shrink greatly, but the possibility of this ever happening is truly slim and, to add, I wouldn't even think of it as true emotion but rather a mirror image spat back by a chatbot.</p>

<p style="text-indent: 2em">Ultimately, AI simply cannot have emotion. It can get close; it can simulate it; it can talk like it does, but at the end of the day, the difference between true human emotion and AI emotion will never close.</p>
